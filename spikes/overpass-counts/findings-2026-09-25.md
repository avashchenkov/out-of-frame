The breakdowns by tag below come from follow-up queries on the same day.

- **An element is not an object.** One site can be several elements: Heizkraftwerk Reuter
  West is two `landuse=industrial` ways with the same name (185569568 and 454730438), and one
  of them is also tagged `power=plant`. A road or a railway is many segments.
- **At these addresses, roads and railways have a ref, not a name.** None of the 146 road and
  rail elements has a `name`. Main lines carry a `ref`: a DB route number such as 6020, or
  `A 100`. The 42 elements at Corinthstraße are three railway lines and the planned A 100
  (`highway=proposed`), plus seven service tracks.
- **Every road or rail element without a ref is a service track** (`service=siding`, `yard`,
  `spur` or `crossover`): 18 of 37, 7 of 42 and 48 of 67. The tags do not say which line or
  yard a track belongs to. At Saganer Straße, 7 of them are yard tracks under construction.
- **Industry has the unnamed objects.** At Freiheit 22, 17 of the 29 industry elements have
  no name: 12 `landuse=industrial` polygons, 2 of them tagged `industrial=scrap_yard`, and 5
  `man_made=works`.
- **The Gatow elements give the closure away.** Both carry `aeroway=aerodrome`, but one is
  named "Ehemaliger Flugplatz Berlin - Gatow" (former airfield).
- **The public Overpass instance is often busy.** It often answered 504 or 429, even to small
  queries, and one run failed after five tries a minute apart. The run passed once the script
  sent one query per address for all classes, as in the architecture, instead of one per
  class.

What this changes:

- The investigation cap is five unclear objects per class, nearest first. It counts objects,
  not elements. It only limits the cost of a run.
- Service tracks stay in `road_rail`: trains run on sidings and spurs too, and make noise
  there. Tracks tagged as out of use (`railway=disused` or `railway=abandoned`) do not match.
  The checklist does not change.
- The stale-fact eval case needs another object than the Gatow airfield.
